Projects

NACA 2412 airfoil study Oct 2026

I wanted to know if a small change to the Cessna 172's wing could make it more efficient. I ran 64 tests in NASA FoilSim (4 cambers, 4 thicknesses, 4 angles of attack), then flew the best three shapes against the stock wing in climb, cruise and approach.

The best shape in round one (NACA 2408, L/D of 14.46 vs 12.06 for the stock wing) only won at cruise, by 4.7%. The other two lost everywhere. Lesson learned: one test condition doesn't tell you much, and the 1930s design is pretty hard to beat for a trainer. I turned it into a site with a wing lab and a little guessing game.

Jexpresso website Oct–Nov 2026

Jexpresso is a Julia code for simulating fluids and atmospheres on CPUs and GPUs, made by Professor Simone Marras's research group at NJIT. As more people started using it, Professor Marras asked me to build a website to showcase it, since all they had was the GitHub page and the technical docs.

I built the whole site: a home page that explains what Jexpresso does, an install guide, a gallery of their simulations (clouds, shock waves, magnetic fields and more) that you can click to see full size, and a page for the team and how to cite the code. I kept it plain HTML and CSS on purpose so anyone in the group can edit it, and I keep adding to it, like new gallery sections.

ClearDose Nov 2026

For the STEM2Connect AI Hackathon. You take a photo of a prescription label and it explains it in plain words, in your own language. "Take 1 tab PO BID" becomes "take 1 tablet by mouth, 2 times a day." It can read the explanation out loud and set reminders, and if the photo is too blurry it tells you to ask your pharmacist instead of guessing.

About me

I've always wanted to know how things actually work, and for me that's mostly airplanes. Why is a wing shaped the way it is? Could a small change make it better? My airfoil study started with exactly those questions, and the answer (mostly "no, the 1930s engineers got it right") taught me more than a win would have.

Working with the Jexpresso group has been my first look at real research software. I'm learning Julia, how meshes for simulations are made, and how a team keeps a big codebase organized and documented. Building their website was a good way to give something back while I learn.

I like projects that help people directly too. ClearDose came from thinking about how confusing medicine labels are, especially if English isn't your first language.
